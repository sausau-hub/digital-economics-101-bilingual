LESSON 02 / 11

The Digital Economy

数字经济

Over the past decade, there has been a big shift in how we do work. More opportunities have opened up, remote work is common, and technological advancements have begun to replace labor-intensive jobs with creative work.

There is more opportunity than ever, but only for those that are open to shedding their prior beliefs on what “work” is.

During this shift, you may have picked up beliefs that make you think:

在过去的十年里，我们的工作方式发生了巨大的变化。机会更加开放，远程工作变得普遍，技术进步已经开始用创造性工作取代劳动密集型工作。

现在的机会比以往任何时候都多，但只有那些愿意放下“工作应该是什么样”这一旧观念的人，才能抓住这些机会。

在这场转变中，你可能已经形成了一些让你产生以下想法的信念：

  • You need startup capital
  • You need a lot of experience
  • You need a lot of followers
  • You need a physical product or service location
  • You need to avoid competition and saturation
  • You need to be neurotic about profit margins, operating costs, and supply chains

All of this could not be further from the truth.

If you are more exposed and open to the digital economy, you may also think:

  • 你需要启动资金
  • 你需要大量经验
  • 你需要大量的追随者
  • 你需要一个实体产品或服务场所
  • 你需要避免竞争和市场饱和
  • 你必须为利润率、运营成本和供应链而神经紧绷

所有这些都与事实相去甚远。

如果你对数字经济更加了解和开放，你可能还会认为：

  • You have to be extremely good looking to build an audience
  • You need to be insightful, wise, and produce Jordan Peterson-level ideas
  • You have to be a skilled writer, speaker, and video creator, and possess all of the fancy equipment that is reserved for those that have “made it”

Again, none of these are true.

All of this really is as simple as it may seem, but we all know how hard that can be.

In the coming sections and modules, I will be helping you shed these limiting beliefs while giving you simple systems to help you thrive in this new economy.

  • 你必须长得非常好看才能吸引受众
  • 你需要有洞察力、有智慧，能产生乔丹·彼得森级别的深刻见解
  • 你必须是一名熟练的写作者、演说家和视频创作者，并拥有那些“功成名就”之人才配拥有的高端设备

同样，这些也都不是真的。

这一切其实真的就像看起来那样简单，但我们都知道，简单不等于容易。

在接下来的章节和模块中，我将帮助你摆脱这些限制性信念，同时给你一套简单的系统，帮助你在这个新经济中茁壮成长。

Assumptions Are Your Worst Enemy假设是你最大的敌人

If you have not experienced a common saying or piece of advice — you have no business adopting that as a hardset belief or assumption.

Open your mind, test it out, and find out for yourself whether it is true or not.

如果你自己没有亲身验证过一句俗语或一条建议，就没有理由把它当作坚定不移的信念或假设照单全收。

打开你的思维，亲自测试一下，自己去验证它到底是不是真的。

The Reality Of The Online World We Have Created我们所创造的网络世界的现实

This course is focused on the digital aspect of all of this.

While physical products are great — and can 100% be sold from your brand — it’s almost a smarter move to build on a foundation of digital products and services.

本课程侧重于这一切的数字化层面。

虽然实体产品很棒——而且完全可以由你的品牌来销售——但以数字产品和服务作为基础，几乎是更明智的选择。

Why?为什么？

  • They cost close to nothing to create
  • If you’re a one-person business, they can have 90%+ profit margins on the low end
  • They have no replication cost. Once you know they sell, all you have to do is increase the traffic being sent to them.
  • For something like freelance or consulting work, you can decrease the time you spend on client work while building leverage
  • 它们的制作成本几乎为零
  • 如果你是一人企业，利润率在低端也能达到90%以上
  • 它们没有复制成本。一旦你知道产品能卖出去，你要做的就只是增加导向它的流量
  • 对于自由职业或咨询服务这类工作，你可以在建立杠杆的同时，减少花在客户工作上的时间

This gives you complete control over your income.

Want to do more? Put in time or money to build free sources of traffic.

Like an on/off switch for making money.

But, how do you get people to see these products?

Thankfully, you don’t need to invest in a physical location. In fact, you don’t need to invest in anything at all.

Programmers around the world got tired of building websites and landing pages, and made this skill accessible to anyone.

这让你能完全掌控自己的收入。

想做得更多？投入时间或金钱去建立免费的流量来源。

就像一个赚钱的开关按钮。

但是，你要如何让人们看到这些产品呢？

值得庆幸的是，你不需要投资任何实体场所。事实上，你什么都不需要投资。

世界各地的程序员们厌倦了不断搭建网站和落地页，于是把这项技能变成了任何人都能用上的工具。

What About Hosting The Product, Booking Calls, And Setting Up “Storefronts”?托管产品、预约电话和搭建“店铺”呢？

Also free. No-code tools make this possible.

“No-code tools” is just a fancy way of saying software that works for you — like little salespeople that run 24/7 without costing you a dime (until you grow fast enough to warrant investing).

同样免费。无代码工具让这一切成为可能。

所谓“无代码工具”，说白了就是替你工作的软件——就像一群24小时无休的小小销售员，不花你一分钱（直到你成长到值得投资的阶段）。

Things like:

  • Gumroad for hosting ebooks, courses, and memberships
  • Calendly for creating a call calendar and booking link
  • Carrd for building quick landing pages to better sell your offer
  • Revue or ConvertKit for building email lists
  • Website builders for building a personal website
  • Social media platforms can build a traffic source for you just like Facebook ads — but at zero cost if you know how to use the platform

比如：

  • Gumroad 用于托管电子书、课程和会员制内容
  • Calendly 用于创建通话日历和预约链接
  • Carrd 用于快速搭建落地页，更好地销售你的产品
  • Revue 或 ConvertKit 用于搭建邮件列表
  • 建站工具用于搭建个人网站
  • 社交媒体平台能像 Facebook 广告一样为你建立流量来源——但只要你懂得如何使用这个平台，成本为零

All of them are free, and this is just a portion of them. Most of them only become a paid investment once you’re making enough money to justify it.

它们都是免费的，而这还只是其中一部分。它们中的大多数，只有当你已经赚到足够多的钱来证明投资的必要性时，才会变成需要付费的选项。

But Dan, What About Experience?但是丹，经验怎么办？

Don’t I have to be some kind of super-skilled expert in a specific field?

Well, yes, but there’s a way around this. In fact, it’s most beneficial to start as a beginner.

A big part of building an audience is educating your audience toward the goal they want to reach. They want to become a more valuable person through these methods. This puts you one step ahead of them.

Who else is more worth learning from than someone that is 1-2 steps ahead of you?

Someone 10 steps ahead of you won’t give you the right advice. They’re operating on a completely different playing field.

But someone one step ahead of you knows how to navigate whatever obstacle you’re facing.

There are tactics and strategies that will help you grow rapidly as a beginner.

难道我不需要在某个特定领域成为超级技能高手或专家吗？

嗯，是的，但这是有办法绕过去的。事实上，从初学者开始反而更有利。

建立受众的重要一环，就是引导你的受众达成他们想要的目标——他们想通过这些方法让自己变得更有价值，而这恰恰让你走在了他们前面一步。

还有谁比领先你1-2步的人更值得你学习呢？

领先你10步的人给不了你正确的建议，因为他们身处完全不同的赛场。

但领先你一步的人，才知道该如何应对你正面临的那个障碍。

作为初学者，有一些战术和策略能帮助你快速成长。

Example: teaching what you love learning

Let’s say you listened to a podcast about marketing.

You create high-performing content about it (teaching people what you learned).

You then tag the podcaster you learned it from. Why?

举个例子：教授你自己热爱学习的内容

假设你听了一期关于营销的播客。

你就这个话题创作出高质量的内容（把你学到的东西教给别人）。

然后你在内容里标记出你学习内容的那位播客主。为什么要这么做？

  • Citing your sources makes your argument more credible (authority)
  • The podcaster may like, comment, or retweet it — meaning you could gain followers from their large audience
  • The podcaster will remember you, especially if the content is good — meaning you may work with them in the future
  • 引用你的信息来源会让你的观点更可信（权威性）
  • 播客主可能会点赞、评论或转发——这意味着你有可能从他们庞大的受众中获得新的关注者
  • 播客主会记住你，尤其是当内容质量很高时——这意味着你未来可能有机会与他们合作

I’ve worked with YouTubers and podcasters since I was a kid listening to their work. When you understand that everyone online is a normal person just like you — they’ve just arranged consistent representation that makes them look like some sort of celebrity.

When we talk about learning in public, we’ll go over this zero-experience strategy in more depth.

从小听着 YouTuber 和播客主的内容长大的我，后来真的和他们中的许多人合作过。当你明白网络上的每个人其实都和你一样只是个普通人——他们只是通过持续、统一的形象展示，让自己看起来像某种名人而已。

当我们谈到“公开学习”时，会更深入地讲解这个零经验策略。

From Zero To Infinity从零到无限

You can start from zero.

You can scale to infinity.

Don’t let any idea that’s been told to you live rent-free in your mind. Better yet, if you have an idea of your own, write it down online and understand it more deeply.

你可以从零开始。

你可以扩展到无限大。

不要让别人灌输给你的想法，免费占据你的头脑。更好的做法是：如果你有了自己的想法，就把它写下来发布到网上，从而更深入地理解它。

The Age Of Information信息时代

One big thing that many people miss is that information isn’t inherently valuable.

This shouldn’t have to be said, but everything we do as human beings is process information. Literally.

As we go about life, we process information from our experiences — it either helps us live a better or worse life.

This is why we spend more time than we should immersed in educational, motivational, and entertainment content on social media and television.

很多人忽略的一个重要事实是：信息本身并不天然具有价值。

这本不该需要特意强调，但我们人类所做的一切，实际上都是在处理信息——真的，字面意义上如此。

在我们生活的过程中，会不断处理来自各种经历的信息，它要么让我们的生活变得更好，要么变得更糟。

这也是为什么我们会花费比应有更多的时间，沉浸在社交媒体和电视上的教育性、激励性和娱乐性内容之中。

This isn’t hard to notice.

If you follow me on social media and similar accounts, you’re probably well aware of this.

You can learn more practical information from watching 20 hours of design-related YouTube videos than a student who spent 4 years earning a design degree.

This applies to any subject — not just design, or the industry you’ve been exposed to.

这一点其实不难察觉。

如果你在社交媒体等平台关注我，应该对此深有体会。

你花20个小时看与设计相关的 YouTube 视频，学到的实用信息可能比一个读了4年设计学位的学生还要多。

这个道理适用于任何领域，不只是设计，也不只是你曾接触过的行业。

Small “boutique” brands with a team of about 3 people are pulling in 7 figures a year.

A personal brand that knows what it’s doing can make $20K a month working just 4 hours a week.

As you’ll learn, your personal brand is what sets you apart.

Now think about it.

Do you really want to have a huge team and a billion-dollar company?

Or do you want to do what you love, and at the end of the day make a million dollars a year (or however much you want), living the life you’ve envisioned for yourself?

只有约3人团队的小型“精品”品牌，一年也能做到7位数收入。

一个懂得自己在做什么的个人品牌，每周只花4个小时工作，也能实现每月2万美元的收入。

正如你将学到的，个人品牌正是让你脱颖而出的地方。

现在不妨想一想。

你真的想拥有一支庞大的团队和一家价值十亿美元的公司吗？

还是你更想做自己喜欢的事情，最终一年赚到一百万美元（或者任何你想要的数字），过上你所设想的生活？

To me, it’s obvious.

My job is to educate, entertain, and inspire my audience.

However, I’m not creating followers — I’m creating new leaders who can evolve just as I have.

This is one thing you’ll learn — individual evolution.

The information, products, and services I’ve provided have evolved over time. I moved on, and that opened up space for others to take my place.

对我来说，答案是显而易见的。

我的工作是教育、娱乐和激励我的受众。

然而，我创造的不是追随者，而是能够像我一样不断进化的新一代引领者。

这是你将学到的一件事——个体的进化。

我过去提供的信息、产品和服务，都随着时间不断演变；当我向前迈进，也就为其他人腾出了空间，让他们可以接过接力棒。

Take Responsibility承担责任

Focus your attention on helping yourself and your community.

Pay no mind to those that don’t align with your message.

Pay all your attention to those you can impact the most — encourage them to spread your message — and start leaving your mark on the world.

把你的注意力集中在帮助自己和你的社区上。

不要理会那些和你的理念不一致的人。

全心全意地关注那些你能产生最大影响的人——鼓励他们传播你的信息——并开始在这个世界上留下属于你的印记。
